Korea Decode is that same conversation, written down: which neighbourhood suits your trip, what a bowl of kalguksu should cost, which tour is worth booking and which one is a bus ride with a microphone.

“The menu just says 순두부. What do I do?”
Sundubu-jjigae — soft tofu stew, usually 9,000–12,000 KRW (about $7–9). It arrives boiling, so give it a minute. You will normally be asked how spicy you want it and whether you want an egg cracked in; “순한 망게” (mild) is a safe answer. Rice and side dishes come with it and refills of the side dishes are free. It is one of the few Korean stews commonly served as a single portion, so it works well if you are eating alone.

What is Korea Decode?

Korea Decode is a practical English-language guide to South Korea. Not a culture blog, not a news feed — a set of guides built to answer the questions people actually have before and during a trip: what to book, how to get there, what it costs, what to order, and what to bring home.

Every guide falls into one of four buckets. Book covers tickets, tours, and passes worth reserving ahead. Plan covers routes, transport, timing, and etiquette. Shop covers K-Beauty, tax refunds, and souvenirs. Eat covers ordering, prices, spice levels, and where to go when it is midnight and you are hungry.

Why Korea Decode?

Most English-language Korea content is written far from Korea. It ages badly: prices drift, restaurants move, tours get discontinued, and the “hidden gem” in the listicle has a ninety-minute queue. Korea Decode is written in Seoul, and guides are revisited when the facts on the ground change.

The format is deliberately plain. Each guide opens with a short answer for readers who only want the recommendation, then backs it up with a comparison table, specific costs in won and dollars, the nearest subway station and exit, and the caveats that decide whether something suits your trip. Where a tour or product can be booked online, the link is there — labelled, and disclosed.

If you are planning a first trip to Seoul, working out whether Busan is worth two days or four, rebuilding your skincare shelf from Olive Young, or trying to read a menu that has no pictures, start with the four categories above. If something is still unclear, Decode This will answer it directly.
