Korean Etiquette Guide: Mastering Manners, Bowing & Dining Rules

Mon Jan 26 2026

Photo by Brother Yoon on Unsplash

Annyeonghaseyo, Decode Fam! If you’re new to Seoul, you’ve probably noticed that things run on a beautiful, yet specific, set of social rules. I remember my first week here—I was handing things over with one hand and almost caused an international incident! Don’t sweat it. Korean etiquette, or Yeh-eui (예의), is all about showing respect, and once you know the basics, you unlock a whole new level of cultural connection. Let’s decode this essential guide to Korean manners!

Understanding Korean Hierarchy and Respect (Two-Handed Gestures)

The biggest hurdle for most expats is understanding the concept of hierarchy and respect for elders (Sunbae/Hoobae or just older people). This influences almost every interaction, from how you speak to how you handle objects.

  • Two Hands are Better Than One: When you hand anything important (money, a card, or a gift) to an older person or someone of higher status, use both hands. When receiving, often two hands are used, or at least the left hand supports the right arm/wrist. It’s a huge sign of deference and a core piece of Korean etiquette!
  • The Drinking Game Rule: Never, ever pour your own drink at a dinner table. It’s considered bad luck or selfish! Wait for someone else to pour for you, and always pour for others (starting with the eldest). When receiving a drink from an elder, hold the glass with two hands and slightly turn your head away to take the first sip.
  • The Bowing Spectrum: A quick head nod is fine for peers and casual greetings, but deeper, waist-level bows are reserved for formal situations, meeting elders, or showing sincere gratitude. Just follow the local lead—they won’t mind if you’re a bit clumsy with your Korean bowing technique!

The Philosophy Behind Korean Etiquette (Yeh-eui)

Okay, the system of Yeh-eui might seem intense at first, but honestly? I love how clear and intentional Korean etiquette is. Unlike in the West where you might second-guess if you were rude, here, the rules create a predictable rhythm. It’s not about judging you; it’s about showing concrete care and respect for the relationship. It smooths out social interactions and makes everything feel appreciated. It’s incredibly heartwarming when a Korean friend uses slightly less formal language with me because we’ve built trust—that feels like a true sign of friendship!

Essential Korean Dining Rules and Everyday Manners (Quick Tips)

Here are a few rapid-fire tips that will make you look like a seasoned Seoulite and master your Korean table manners:

  • The Silent Sniffle: Blowing your nose loudly in public, especially at the dinner table, is generally considered uncouth. If you need to clear your sinuses, excuse yourself to the restroom!
  • Spoons vs. Chopsticks: The spoon (sutgarak) is traditionally for rice and soup. Chopsticks (jeotgarak) are for side dishes (banchan) and meat. Do NOT stick chopsticks upright in your rice bowl—that resembles funerary rites!
  • Subway Seating: See those brightly colored seats near the doors on the subway? Those are priority seats for the elderly, pregnant women, or disabled. Even if the train is packed, try to keep them free!
  • Dining Initiation: Always wait for the oldest person at the table to pick up their utensils before you start eating. It’s a small, easy sign of respect.

Keep Practicing, Keep Decoding Korean Etiquette!

Listen, nobody expects you to be perfect overnight. Koreans are incredibly forgiving of foreigners who are genuinely trying. The effort you put into understanding Yeh-eui and mastering Korean manners is noticed and deeply appreciated. So go forth, bow slightly, use those two hands, and enjoy navigating this incredible culture!
